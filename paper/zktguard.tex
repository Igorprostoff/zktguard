\begin{abstract}
\sys{} is an open-source research project that turns Telegram's
existing trust signals into privacy-preserving on-chain credentials on
TON. A proxy attestor witnesses the response to a Telegram-shaped HTTP
endpoint, signs the transcript with EdDSA over BabyJubjub, and a
Groth16 circuit over BLS12-381 attests in zero knowledge that the
transcript decrypts to a JSON object whose ``account creation''
timestamp predates a public threshold. A FunC verifier on TON checks
the proof using the chain's BLS pairing precompile, records a
per-application nullifier, and (in the next iteration) mints a
soulbound credential NFT. We describe the construction, the v0.1
reference implementation, the constraints we had to relax to stay
inside a circuit-friendly arithmetic, and a roadmap towards a fully
realised version of the design.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Telegram Mini Apps host hundreds of millions of users on TON. Sybil
resistance for these apps is an unsolved problem: existing identity
systems (Human Passport, World ID, Polygon ID) ignore the
platform-native trust signals that already exist inside Telegram.
\sys{} is, to the authors' knowledge, the first published design that
uses Telegram itself as a credential issuer via zkTLS.

The design has four moving parts: a proxy attestor (a stateless Go
service), a prover (a stateless Rust service), a FunC verifier
contract, and a soulbound credential contract. A client-side Mini App
connects them via TON Connect 2.0. The bundle is published under MIT,
with no hosted service, no telemetry, no fee handling, and no token.

This paper describes the construction (\S\ref{sec:construction}), the
reference implementation (\S\ref{sec:impl}), an evaluation
(\S\ref{sec:eval}), the limitations of v0.1 (\S\ref{sec:limits}), and
the open questions we believe v1 should address (\S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Proof systems.} Groth16~\cite{Groth16} remains the canonical
choice for circuits with stable verification keys. Universal-setup
systems --- PLONK, Plonky3~\cite{Plonky3}, Halo2, and Binius~\cite{Binius}
--- avoid per-circuit ceremonies but pay a runtime cost. v0.1 picks
Groth16 because the on-chain verifier reduces to a single
BLS\_PAIRING; a universal-setup variant is specified in
\S\ref{sec:future}.

\paragraph{Proxy attestation.} Reclaim Protocol~\cite{Reclaim} pioneered
the modern proxy-attestation construction we re-use here: a notary
forwards TLS records, signs the encrypted transcript, and the prover
later proves in zero knowledge that a witnessed key decrypts to the
required field. TLSNotary~\cite{TLSNotary} provides a stronger,
MPC-TLS variant that we view as the natural v1 upgrade.

\paragraph{Identity primitives.} Semaphore~\cite{Semaphore},
zkLogin~\cite{zkLogin}, Sui zkLogin~\cite{SuiZkLogin}, and
World ID~\cite{WorldID} all build credentials atop a non-revealing
identity primitive. \sys{} reuses the same idea --- nullifiers as the
on-chain primary key --- and adapts it to the Telegram + TON
deployment surface.

\paragraph{Folded proofs.} Nova~\cite{Nova} and HyperNova-style folding
schemes offer a path to session continuity that the v0 per-action
proof does not yet exploit. We treat them as future work
(\S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Cryptographic construction}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Setup}

Let $r$ be the order of the BLS12-381 scalar field, $G$ a generator of
$\mathbb{G}_1$, $H$ a generator of $\mathbb{G}_2$, and $e:
\mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \to \mathbb{G}_T$ a Type-3 Ate pairing.
A trusted setup produces $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, \tau) \in
(\mathbb{F}_r^\times)^5$ and a chain of $\mathrm{IC}_i \in \mathbb{G}_1$
for $i \in \{0, \dots, n\}$ where $n$ is the number of public inputs.

\subsection{Verification equation}

The on-chain verifier accepts a proof
$\pi = (A, B, C) \in \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \times \mathbb{G}_1$
and a public-input vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_r^n$ iff
\begin{equation}
e(A, B) \;=\; e(\alpha G,\, \beta H) \cdot e\!\big(\mathrm{vk}_x,\, \gamma H\big) \cdot e(C,\, \delta H),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{vk}_x \;=\; \mathrm{IC}_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot \mathrm{IC}_i.
\end{equation}
The FunC verifier reduces this to a single four-pair pairing check:
\begin{equation}
e(A, B) \cdot e(-\alpha G,\, \beta H) \cdot e(-\mathrm{vk}_x,\, \gamma H) \cdot e(-C,\, \delta H) \;=\; 1.
\end{equation}
TON's \texttt{BLS\_PAIRING} opcode computes the product directly.

\subsection{Public inputs}

The v0.1 circuit publishes eight scalars in this order: \texttt{nonce},
\texttt{app\_id}, \texttt{expiration}, \texttt{claim\_type},
\texttt{nullifier}, \texttt{attestor\_pubkey\_x},
\texttt{attestor\_pubkey\_y}, and \texttt{threshold\_months}. The
high 32 bits of \texttt{nonce} carry the \texttt{chain\_id} so the
verifier can enforce replay protection without a separate public
input.

\subsection{Nullifier}

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{nullifier} = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3\!\big(\mathit{user\_secret},\, \mathit{app\_id},\, \mathit{claim\_type}\big).
\end{equation}
Per-application, per-claim, deterministic, unlinkable across
\texttt{app\_id} for the same user.

\subsection{Constraint groups}

The \texttt{account\_age} circuit has six sub-templates. Two are
implemented in v0.1 (\texttt{TimestampThreshold},
\texttt{NullifierPoseidon}); one is implemented for BabyJubjub EdDSA
verification (\texttt{AttestorEdDSAVerify}); three remain stubs in v0.1
(\texttt{AEADDecrypt}, \texttt{TranscriptCommitment},
\texttt{JsonTimestampExtract}). The stubs land alongside Task 3 (the
trusted-setup ceremony) in v0.2.

\subsection{Trust model}

The construction follows the proxy-attestation model. The attestor
must not collude with the prover. v0.1 demonstrates with a single
test attestor; the architecture admits a future attestor set with
diverse operators using a threshold or set-membership check on chain.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:impl}

The reference implementation is a monorepo containing a Go attestor
(\texttt{attestor/}), a Rust prover (\texttt{prover/}), four FunC
contracts (\texttt{contracts/}), a Circom circuit
(\texttt{circuits/}), a TypeScript SDK (\texttt{sdk/}), a Mini App
(\texttt{miniapp/}), and three example Mini Apps
(\texttt{examples/}). All components are MIT-licensed.

\paragraph{Attestor.} Stateless Go binary. Hashes the upstream
response with Poseidon over BN254 and signs with EdDSA on BabyJubjub
using \texttt{iden3/go-iden3-crypto}. Exposes \texttt{/attest},
\texttt{/pubkey}, and \texttt{/health}.

\paragraph{Prover.} Stateless Rust binary. v0.1 emits \emph{synthetic}
proofs against a placeholder VK whose scalars are public knowledge,
so end-to-end sandbox tests exercise the on-chain verifier without
having a real trusted-setup output yet. The CLI is
\texttt{zktguard-prover prove --input <json> --output <proof.json>}.

\paragraph{Verifier.} FunC contract. Embeds the (placeholder) VK as
compile-time constants. Reads a proof and a public-input snake from
the message body, computes $\mathrm{vk}_x$ via two
\texttt{BLS\_G1\_MULTIEXP} calls joined by \texttt{BLS\_G1\_ADD}
(FunC's asm directive caps positional args at 16), and invokes
\texttt{BLS\_PAIRING} for the four-pair check. Records used
nullifiers in a 256-bit dictionary.

\paragraph{Soulbound credential.} A TEP-62-shaped Collection +
non-transferable Item pair. Transfers throw exit \texttt{410};
non-verifier mints throw exit \texttt{411}.

\paragraph{App registry.} Stand-alone contract storing the canonical
set of accepted \texttt{(app\_id, claim\_type)} pairs in a 96-bit
dictionary. Admin-gated mutations.

\paragraph{SDK and Mini App.} Pure TypeScript, browser-safe,
no Buffer dependency. Bundle is well under 50 KB gzipped.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{On-chain cost}

Sandbox measurements taken via \texttt{@ton/sandbox} v0.32 on
\texttt{@ton/core} 0.62. Testnet numbers will replace these once
deployment lands (see \texttt{contracts/DEPLOYMENTS.md}).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
Operation                                & Gas (sandbox) \\\hline
Pairing sanity check, good pair          & 62\,741 \\
Pairing sanity check, bad pair           & 62\,625 \\
Groth16 verify, accept path              & 148\,881 \\
Soulbound mint                           & TBD \\
Registry register                        & TBD \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Off-chain cost (synthetic prover)}

The v0.1 prover does scalar arithmetic plus three fixed-base
multiplications and one inverse. On a 2024 Apple~M2 in release mode
the inner \texttt{prove()} call runs in tens of microseconds; the
binary spends most of its wall time on JSON parsing.

\subsection{Circuit constraints (forecast)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \texttt{NullifierPoseidon}: $\sim 300$ constraints.
  \item \texttt{TimestampThreshold}: $\sim 1\,000$ constraints.
  \item \texttt{AttestorEdDSAVerify}: $\sim 4\,000$ constraints.
  \item \texttt{AEADDecrypt}: tens of thousands of constraints once
    AES-128-GCM lands.
  \item \texttt{TranscriptCommitment} + \texttt{JsonTimestampExtract}:
    folded into the AEAD body.
\end{itemize}
v0.1 reports an empty constraint total because the heavy groups are
stubbed.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Placeholder VK.} The verifier contract embeds a placeholder
verification key. Anyone with the VK can craft an accepting proof.
This is acceptable for v0.1's sandbox demonstrations but precludes
mainnet deployment.

\paragraph{Attestor curve.} BabyJubjub EdDSA, not BLS12-381 as the
bible's Part C originally specifies. BLS verification inside a
Groth16/bn128 circuit costs millions of constraints; BabyJubjub clocks
in around 4\,000.

\paragraph{No on-chain registry lookup.} The verifier hard-codes
acceptance of \texttt{(1, 1)} in v0.1. A cross-contract send to the
App Registry is the natural follow-up; an admin-pushed snapshot is
the alternative.

\paragraph{No real trusted setup.} The Powers-of-Tau choice is
documented in \texttt{circuits/PTAU.md} but no Phase-2 contribution has
been performed.

\paragraph{No real Telegram traffic.} v0.1 runs against a stub server.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Future work}\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Migrate proxy attestation to MPC-TLS (TLSNotary) once that
    ecosystem matures.
  \item Replace the per-action proof with folded proofs (Nova,
    HyperNova) for session continuity.
  \item Standardise the claim catalog beyond v0's
    \texttt{account\_age}. Draft schema invites community
    contribution rather than freezing one curve.
  \item Cross-credential reputation: compose multiple verified claims
    into a higher-assurance attestation.
  \item Formal security proof of the proxy-attestation construction
    against an adversarial Mini App author.
  \item Alternative claim sources beyond Telegram, using the same
    architecture with different proxy attestor configurations.
  \item Mobile-side proving benchmarks across Android and iOS, varying
    circuit size.
\end{enumerate}
